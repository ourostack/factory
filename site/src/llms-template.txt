# The factory

> The factory is the public measurement store for Desk, a plugin that gives coding agents a durable desk of tracks and tasks. It measures agentic work the way Lean measures a factory floor: where each task's time went, which part added value, and which part was waste. It names no person, no machine and no content: the only calendar facts are each task's finish day (UTC), times on a task's own clock, and pull request times from GitHub. This file indexes the data behind every view, for agents.

Site: https://ourostack.github.io/factory/
Source and raw data: https://github.com/ourostack/factory (the `reports` branch holds the built reports)

## How to walk it

The site walks the four steps Lean uses to see waste, in this order. Read the data in the same order.

1. Follow a task (`#/task/<job>`): one real task end to end, and where its time went. `#/` opens on the latest-finished task that has something to teach and waste labels, named or private. In `data.json` `jobs[]`: of the tasks whose `status` is `done` or `cancelled` and whose `finish_date` has a `value`, keep those whose `lead_time_ms` is measured, or partial with `bound: "lower"`, at 3600000 ms or more; whose `active_time_ms` is measured, or partial with a value, at 300000 ms or more; `map_bursts` has a value of 1 or more and is not `unavailable`; `human_turns` has a value of 1 or more; and `waste.sessions_labeled` has a value of 1 or more (the task has evaluator waste labels). Open the one with the latest `finish_date.value`. The store knows no order within a day, so tasks with the same day are a tie, and a tie goes to the larger `lead_time_ms.value`. If no task qualifies, the label condition is dropped; if still none qualifies, the page opens the latest-finished task whose `lead_time_ms` is measured, or partial with `bound: "lower"`, at 3600000 ms or more (ties the same way), then the latest dated finished task, then the finished task with the largest `finish_order`. The page says which rule chose the task and why the finished tasks after it in finish order were skipped. `map_bursts` counts the work bursts the task's map draws: `unavailable` when Desk could not tell the bursts, so the map draws none.
2. Compare tasks (`#/compare`): every task side by side on one scale, as a stack-up of working time by label and waiting by cause, in hours of all elapsed time (`?mode=all`), hours of agent working time only (`?mode=working`) or each task's shares of its own lead time on a 0 to 100% axis (`?mode=share`), with each finished task's finish day (UTC) under its bar. Its "Over time" section reads `rollups/by_week.json` and opens with the base it covers (how many finished tasks and lead-time hours are dated) and how many of their finish days are exact, "on or before", "on or after" or of no known direction; it claims a change between two weeks only when no bounded finish day could fall into either: every task in them has an exact day, no "on or before" day lies in them or a later week, no "on or after" day in them or an earlier week, and no day lacks a direction; otherwise it says there are not yet enough exact finish days to compare. It shows, newest week on the right: "By week" (`?over=week`, the default), one stacked bar per finish week in the stack-up's colors, as each week's shares of its own lead time (`?otmode=share`, the default, so a week of 5 tasks and a week of 1 compare), hours of all elapsed time (`?otmode=all`) or hours of agent working time (`?otmode=working`); "Each task" (`?over=task`), the stack-up's dated tasks in finish order in the same modes; a cause × week table (rows are causes, largest first; columns are finish weeks; each cell its hours or share with a bar on one stated scale, and ≥, ≤ or ~ for a partial figure), which scrolls inside its frame, opened at the newest week; flow efficiency by finish week, each task as a dot when measured or as the range its partial value may lie in, with each week's median; and a table of the weeks, newest first. Each week header says how its tasks' days lean, and a week whose tasks may belong to an earlier or later week says so. A finished task with no finish day is counted in one line with its hours, listed behind it with its reason, and never placed on a guessed date.
3. Rank causes (`#/causes`): causes of waste ranked by the time they cost, in a Pareto chart; each cause has its own page (`#/causes/<key>`, for example `#/causes/waiting:next_prompt`) with its tasks, its stretches and a prompt that starts an A3, or, when a kaizen issue already works on that cause, a link to it and a prompt to check or extend it.
4. Act (`#/act`): the problems taken on (kaizen issues, `data.json` `kaizen_issues`), each with its countermeasure, its cause where the site maps one (`kaizen_issues[].cause`), and its check, and the issues that own the status line's alarms.

Reference pages: `#/why` (why Lean, the eight wastes and a glossary), `#/about` (how the data gets here and the privacy rules), `#/store` (everything else the store measures).

## Reading the numbers

Every number is an object `{ state, value?, reasons }`. `measured` is the whole figure. `partial` is part of it, and `bound` says whether the true figure is higher (`lower`) or lower (`upper`); when the direction is not known, `data.json` says `unknown` and every file under `rollups/` and `map/`, Desk's and the store's, says `null`, with `bound_reason` giving why where Desk states it (`reasons.json` has its words). `unavailable` means not measured: its value is absent or null, and it is never zero. `reasons` are codes; `reasons.json` gives the words the page shows for each. {{DIRECTION}} A rollup also carries `n` of `N` and what it counts. Every task has a `finish_date` (`data.json` `jobs[]`, and on each row of `rollups/tasks.json` and each file under `map/`): `{ state, value, basis, bound?, reasons }`, whose `value` is the UTC day the task finished ("YYYY-MM-DD"), never a time of day. `basis` says where the day comes from, and the first source that gives one wins: `desk_transition` (Desk's record of the day the session moved the card to done or cancelled, measured), `desk_card_updated` (the day the card was last updated, so the task finished on or before it: `partial`, `bound: "upper"`), `pr_anchor` (the task's pull requests tie its clock to GitHub's `created_at` for them, and the day is that anchor plus the end of the task's lead window: the anchor is the one that places pull request times on the map: measured when a pull request the session opened sets it or several agree; `partial` with `bound: "lower"` (the task finished on or after the day) and reason `anchor_unconfirmed` when nothing confirms the pull requests it rests on; when they disagree, `partial` with reason `anchor_spread`: with `bound: "lower"` and the added reason `anchor_unconfirmed` when the anchor rests on timed pull requests the session is not known to have opened (each of them is at or before the true start), and with no direction when pull requests the session opened disagree; when the task's lead window is partial only because the card's dates are shorter than the work, the day is that of the last recorded work, `partial`, `bound: "upper"`, reason `finish_from_last_work`; any other partial window gives reason `lead_window_partial` and no direction), `labels_landed` (the UTC day the task's waste labels first landed in the store, so the task finished on or before it: `partial`, `bound: "upper"`, reason `finish_from_labels_landing`; if the task's pull requests put its finish after that day and that anchored day is not itself an "at most" day, the labels day is shown as `partial` with no direction and reason `anchor_after_labels`, because the two disagree (two "at most" days do not disagree: the earlier one, the labels day, stands as `bound: "upper"`); when GitHub could not be read for the anchor, the labels day also carries the reason, for example `github_unreadable` or `github_lookup_capped`). A task still open has no finish day (`open_job`), a task whose status could not be read has `status_unavailable`, a desk that withholds job timing has `job_offsets_withheld`, and a finished task no source gives has `no_finish_source`. Tasks are ordered by `finish_order`: by `finish_date` value, earliest first; ties, and then finished tasks with no date, by the order in which each task's first waste labels landed in the store; open tasks and tasks whose status could not be read last, by their first facts file. In `rollups/` and `map/`, a finish day whose reasons pull both ways (an "at least" anchor and an "at most" last-work day) has `bound: null` and `bound_reason: "bound_reasons_conflict"`. `finish_basis` is `labels` for a task with labels, `date` for a dated task with none yet, and `facts` for the rest. Tasks whose labels landed in the same commit share a `finish_group` and are ordered within it by lead time, so the longest of them takes the latest position. A task's `name` is the title of its earliest-opened public pull request, or null for a private task; `name_basis: "partial"` (in `data.json`) means some of its pull requests could not be read, so an earlier one may exist. The store adds `name`, `finish_order`, `finish_group`, `finish_basis` and `finish_date` to every row of `rollups/tasks.json`. `rollups/by_week.json` (`factory.site.by_week/1`) counts each finished task in the ISO week (UTC, Monday first) of its `finish_date` (`basis: "by_finish_week"`); every week from the first finished task to the last is listed, and a week with no finished task is `{ n: 0 }`, never zero hours. Each sum is a stated number, and `n_partial` counts the tasks in a week that rest on a partial figure. A sum of members whose directions agree takes that direction, and a missing member makes it "at least"; a member whose `bound_reason` is `bound_not_moved` is exact and adds no direction to a sum, while one with any other `bound: null` makes the sum `bound: null` too. `n_unsplit` counts a week's tasks whose time does not split into working and waiting yet (no working time), and `unsplit_reasons` gives why; their hours are in the week's `lead_ms` and in no cause, so a cause's zero in such a week is only "at least", and the page says "not measured for k" instead of "≥0". Every week, empty or not, counts its tasks' finish days: `n_day_measured` (exact), `n_day_on_or_before` (`bound: "upper"`, so a task may have finished in an earlier week), `n_day_on_or_after` (`bound: "lower"`, perhaps a later week) and `n_day_about` (no direction); the four add up to `n`, which equals the length of `jobs`, and each matches the week's tasks' own `finish_date`. A share of a week's lead time carries its own direction, worked out from its part and the lead time: a partial part over a partial lead time has no direction unless the two lean so the ratio provably does. A week's flow efficiency median is over the finished tasks with a flow efficiency figure (`n` of `N`): measured when every figure is; when partial figures all lean one way, the median of the known values is a bound in that direction (for example at most 43%, 4 of 5 tasks); when they lean both ways or have no direction, it is the median of the exact figures only with reason `median_of_subset`, or unavailable with `bound_reasons_conflict` when no figure is exact. A finished task with no finish date is under `unplaced`.

"Waiting" means idle time only: lead time minus working time, when no agent was working on the task. It is split by what it waited on (`waited_on`): `next_prompt` (the agent had stopped for the operator's next prompt), `other_task` (a session of the task was running, but its agent was working on another task; the other task is never named), `api_retry`, `tool_failure`, `long_tool_call`, `queue_before_start`, `no_session` (no session of the task was running) and `unknown` (no agent was working on the task, and the cause was not recorded). A gap's `waited_on` is its largest cause; where Desk gives `gaps[].idle_by_waited_on_ms`, that is the gap's exact split. The evaluator's labels (value-adding, necessary, each waste) describe working time only.

Waiting for the next prompt is split by why the agent stopped (Desk's reports; each key is absent until Desk publishes it, and the page then shows that waiting unsplit and says why is not known yet, never a zero split). The classes, in the order the page stacks them: `stopped_short` (the agent ended its turn although its authorization covered the next step and nothing blocked it), `question` (it needed information only the operator has), `error_limit` (an API error, a rate or usage limit, an output cap or a refusal), `interrupted` (the operator interrupted it), then the human gates `decision`, `approval` and `acceptance`, then `not_known`. Each wait on the operator is listed in `jobs/<job>.json` `timeline.waits[]` and `map/<job>.json` `waits[]`: `{ session, start_ms, end_ms, next_prompt_ms, stop: { end, asks, pending_agents }, why, why_source, confidence, reasons }`, where `stop` holds the mechanical facts of how the turn ended (never any text), `why_source` is `rule` (decided from `stop.end`), `evaluator` (the evaluator's stop label) or `none`, and `reasons` say why a `not_known` why is not known: `not_labeled`, `could_not_tell`, `stop_not_recorded` (no recorded wait holds the time), `outside_own_share` or `not_in_published_facts` (older facts record no stop). A prompt joins the wait it ends by session and `end_ms == at_ms`, and `map/<job>.json` `human_turns[].why` carries it. `gaps[].idle_by_why_ms` and `bursts[].idle_by_why_ms` split each item's next-prompt idle time by why; `rollups/tasks.json` rows carry `next_prompt_by_why_ms` (each class, and `not_known`, a stated number) and `not_known_by_reason_ms`, which together add up to `waiting_by_waited_on_ms.next_prompt`; while any of it is not known each class is partial, `bound: "lower"`, reason `stop_partly_classified` (at least this much; at most this plus the not-known time). Each stack-up row (Compare tasks) carries `next_prompt_by_why_ms` too, and `longest_gap.value.why` names the why that holds most of a task's longest wait. In `rollups/causes.json` the row `waiting:next_prompt` gains `children[]`, its sub-causes `waiting:next_prompt:<why>` with `parent: "waiting:next_prompt"`, which add up to it and are never ranked beside it; each has its own page, `#/causes/waiting:next_prompt:<why>` (for example `#/causes/waiting:next_prompt:stopped_short`), listing every such wait from the tasks' map files with its stop facts, source and confidence, and a prompt that starts an A3. The Pareto chart stacks the `waiting:next_prompt` bar by why ("Split by why", on by default).

## Data files

### Connected process learning

`data.json.learning` and `rollups/learning.json` are the identical, validated
`factory-learning/1` object. `learning.js` publishes the pure browser
`FactoryLearning.buildLearning({data,causes,history,checks})` and
`FactoryLearning.validateLearning(doc)` interfaces; CommonJS exports the same
functions. `history` is `{by_week,evidence}`: `by_week` is the existing
`factory.site.by_week/1` builder output, and `evidence` contains only public
`{job,timeline:{waits,agents}}` inputs. No comparison or grouping is rerun.
The legacy `data.overview`/`rollups/overview.json` contract remains separate
and unchanged. The existing UI has not yet migrated to this new projection.

- `sources.reports` identifies the report generation; `sources.checks`
  retains the independent Desk/facts/reports revisions supplied by the
  canonical checker. `alignment:"same_reports"` means only the report
  revisions agree, not that facts, issues and reports were captured together.
  Different or unavailable report generations limit the overview conclusion.
- `scope.outcome_records` and `accepted_outcomes` come from the full
  `outcomes.signoff` population, including outcomes without timed work.
  `task_reports` counts `data.jobs` rows; `timed_tasks` counts those with a
  recorded-window figure. Neither is the accepted-outcome denominator.
  `attention` and `attention_turns` retain the canonical sample `n`, accepted
  denominator `N`, state, direction and exclusions. No attention ratio is
  reported beside zero accepted outcomes. `capture` and `first_pass_yield`
  retain their own distinct populations and uncertainty.
- `overview` gives one supported conclusion, `finding` and `improvement`
  references when available, material `limits` and an explicit safe `href`.
  No data means `code:"evidence_unavailable"`, not an all-clear conclusion.
- `findings` is bounded to 24 public cause rows, including explicitly
  reported next-prompt sub-causes. Each carries a stated `figure`,
  `population`, `affected_tasks`, up to six representative `examples`,
  source JSON paths and a deep destination. Its population `n` is the
  finished, fully labeled jobs with readable intervals counted by the cause
  builder; `N` is all considered records, including excluded/open jobs.
  `basis:job_hours` in the source means task exposure, not unique factory
  time or utilization. Waiting observations, evaluator-classified waste,
  unclassified work and reported stop classifications stay distinct.
  A tool-kind suffix identifies the evidence for a defect label; it never
  identifies a production stage. Classification is not proof of avoidability.
- `behaviors` asks about review/repair, human stops and team coordination.
  Recorded return counts retain catch-point/sample limits, not a count of
  complete review cycles. Their `by_reason` figures keep agent error,
  changed ask, new information and external causes separate; a changed ask
  is not automatically corrective waste. Stop `observation` is the host's end/asks/pending
  facts; `judgment` separately retains actual why/source/confidence/reasons.
  Older facts with no recorded host stop preserve `observation:null`; this
  does not erase a separately recorded judgment or invent host facts.
  Never infer one from the other. Team root/child counts are published
  topology, not task-exclusive worker allocation. Stage and coordination
  `duration` remain unavailable; each question states its
  `missing_observation` and owning `collection_path`. Examples are bounded,
  not a census of events.
- `evolution.versions` retains every available canonical release grouping,
  including `mixed`, with each measure's population. `evolution.weeks`
  preserves all available finish weeks, their time/class/waste figures,
  task points, bounded finish dates, empty weeks, unsplit tasks and
  `unplaced` jobs. Its weekly `production_ms` is unavailable: `lead_ms`
  is the distinct recorded window, never a substitute production clock.
  Task mix, shared workers, changed capture and bounded finish dates prevent
  a before/after causal claim. No history is interpolated.
- `improvements` summarizes the canonical `data.improvement_checks`
  (`factory-improvement-checks/1`). Each row links its exact `source_path`
  there and retains `card`, `sample_jobs`, exclusions, before/after jobs,
  independent groups, median states, change and interval with capture
  bounds. A missing median is unavailable; an observed zero is measured or
  partial zero. The interval is the canonical bootstrap interval, not a
  causal guarantee. A partial difference/interval has unknown capture bound.
  `status:"checked",verdict:"too_few_jobs"` is a completed **inconclusive**
  result in `section:"results_available"`; it is not a missing check.
  `waiting_for_version` and `no_check` are `awaiting_result`; malformed
  (`refused`) or unavailable checks remain explicit under `investigating`.
  Checked `confirmed`, `not_confirmed` and `no_clear_change` retain the
  hypothesis-relative results better, worse and no detected change.
  GitHub `issue_state` and `implementation.merged` are separate observations,
  never the measurement result or proof that a change helped.

Only the producer's explicit public `kaizen_issues[].cause` mapping can
connect a finding and an improvement (`association:"mapped_measure_only"`).
These examples are not original triggering jobs; `original_evidence` is
`not_published`. Unknown public/private review associations are not guessed.
No issue bodies, titles, prompts, local paths, session associations or
private decision prose are copied into this projection.

Handoffs identify `file:"rollups/learning.json"`, an exact JSON selector,
a safe existing local `href` and
`authority_limit:"inspect_only_no_new_authority"`. For example, inspect
`$.findings[?(@.id=="waiting:next_prompt")]`, its example's `map_path`,
`start_ms`/`end_ms` task offsets and `source_path` in `rollups/causes.json`;
then inspect the related improvement at
`$.improvements.rows[?(@.ref=="ourostack/factory#53")]` and its canonical
`data.json` check. A map's existing period selector must be resolved from
that map, not invented from a source-list index. Routes currently point at
the supported cause/task/compare/improvements destinations; no future UI
route or execution authority is implied.

The exact top-level `data.learning` subtree has a closed numeric/privacy
validator: counts, populations, offsets, bounds, comparison conservation,
source revisions, fixed text, enums and destinations are checked before
publication. Unknown fields or invalid projections refuse publication
without overwriting the last good twin. Other data and nested subtrees
retain the ordinary number gate; there is no general numeric exemption.
`validateLearning` returns `{ok:false,codes:["learning_projection_invalid"]}`
without echoing rejected content. Browser consumers should withhold only
the learning panel when validation fails, keeping unrelated views usable.

### Improvement annotations: inspect, do not execute

`data.json.improvements` and `rollups/improvements.json` contain the same
`factory-improvements/1` queue. This is a separate closed transport/presentation
contract, not a stated measurement: bounded integer counts are known only
under `coverage.state:"known"`; unknown coverage has null counts. The exact
top-level queue is validated separately by the maintained numbers gate.
Other measurements retain the stated-number rules above.

No batches means `state:"not_reviewed"`. Highest revisions win; equal-revision
or shared-public-issue conflicts remain explicit. Omission is never withdrawal.
Publication freshness is at most 72 hours, not an exact review timestamp;
incomplete/conflicting coverage is unknown, never summed across machines.

`runner` is the publishing machine's own report of its triage runner, from
the same newest fresh batch as coverage: `state` is one of Desk's runner
states (`ran`, `no_agent_cli`, `timeout` and so on) and `last_success_age` is
`recent`, `aging`, `stale`, `never` or `unknown`. It is
`{state:"unknown",last_success_age:"unknown"}` with no single fresh source,
with any invalid batch file, or when the newest batches of the one generation
share a commit time and disagree about the runner. It is a coarse age, not
a time, and a runner that ran says nothing about any one row.

Rows carry `availability`, fixed `gate_explanation`, public `context`, specific
`decision`/`recommendation`/`safe_continuation` availability, `next_action` and
`handoff`. With no verified matching public context, decision details are
private or not published. Current public issue/PR/job sources do not supply
validated specific decisions or continuations: unavailable fields are
`{state:"unavailable",reason:"detail_not_published"}`, never guessed from titles.
Ownership not published is unknown.

Known limit of this version: the store verifies no public context. A public
issue, pull request or job report carries no revision the store can check
against a pointer's revision, so the build passes no public evidence to the
queue. Every row therefore reads `private_detail_not_published` or
`stale_or_conflicting` and has an empty `context`, even when its evidence
points at a public issue. `verified_public_context` is defined but not
produced.

The action is “Ask your linked agent to inspect the local annotation.”
The handoff contains the public opaque annotation ID/revision and public
basis/pointers, not a canonical card key or local path. Its authority limit is
`inspect_only_no_new_authority`, even for stale/conflicting rows. Ask the linked
agent to resolve the acknowledged ID on its own desk, inspect authorized local
detail and bring a recommendation to the actual decision owner. Missing or
stale resolution is an honest inability to resolve, not a reason to guess a
card, publish private details, claim work or begin implementation.

Paths are relative to the site. A file up to {{READ_WHOLE}} is marked small enough to read whole.

### Factory briefing and evidence navigation (consumer milestone)

`#/` is Overview; `#/tasks`, `#/compare`, `#/improvements` and `#/why` are directly usable. `#/help` replays the optional three-stop guide. Existing task/session/cause links and `#/act` remain supported. Production and request clocks are shown where Desk publishes them. Triage reviews appear in the improvements queue only when Desk has published them; `state:"not_reviewed"` means none has.

`data.json.overview` and `rollups/overview.json` are identical `factory-overview/1` projections with `generation`, `attention`, `agents`, `investigation`, `changes`, `state` and `reasons`. The overview is a closed public presentation contract checked separately at this exact top-level path; other measurements remain governed by the stated-number checks. Work/quality, collection and improvement-system problems remain distinct. Alarms precede suggested investigations. Missing monitoring is unknown, never all-clear. A triage route cannot create ownership, activity or execution authority.

Rows expose a public ID, family, title, observed-impact limitation, ownership availability, next action and validated local destination; optional figures retain number states. Triage context is copied from the validated Node2 queue, with its opaque public handoff and inspection-only authority. Private decision/recommendation/continuation remain unavailable. Do not infer them from gate codes or issue titles.

Display preferences are versioned and origin-local. Browser storage failure does not break navigation or imply persistence. Since-last-visit text compares only compatible bounded public briefing snapshots, never a complete change history. Selected IANA zones apply only to supported instants. Published finish days and finish weeks remain UTC; relative offsets and durations are invariant. Missing lifecycle clocks are unavailable, not legacy lead relabeled as production.

Lifecycle clocks: each task carries `clocks` in `data.json` `jobs[]` and on its row of the task and stack-up rollups and its map file, where those are published, with the same values in every twin. Desk computes them; the site only words them. Each clock is a number object as above: `production_ms` (production elapsed time: from the first recorded work of each production episode to its recorded delivery, summed over episodes, leaving out the time between deliveries), `active_in_production_ms` (recorded working inside production: the union of this task's recorded agent activity, so overlapping workers count once), `production_remainder_ms` (production time not recorded as active: not proof of idle time or waste, because some work is not captured), `request_to_delivery_ms` (from the original request to recorded delivery) and `queue_ms` (from the original request to the first recorded work). `production_periods` lists the episodes as `{ start_ms, end_ms, so_far? }` offsets on the task's own clock; the gap between one episode's end and the next one's start is time between deliveries, which is neither production time nor an exact acceptance duration. A value of a task still open carries `so_far: true` and may grow. When Desk has no record of the original request, `request_to_delivery_ms` and `queue_ms` are `unavailable` with reason `original_request_not_recorded`: never zero, and never the legacy lead time relabelled. The acceptance wait stays its own coarse class (`signoff_wait`: under one hour, under one day, under seven days, seven days or more), outside production time. On Compare, the production and request modes draw one bar per task; their sums are task exposure (the same hour counts once for every task open in it), not factory time, and no ratio of them is a utilization figure. A ratio of figures with different bounds keeps its uncertainty rather than taking a direction.
